# Core argument

Recorded 2026-11-25.

Functional connectivity is usually read as a portrait of the cortex: its wiring, its
geometry, its intrinsic modes. That reading rests on an assumption rarely stated, that the
cortex is driven by input with no structure of its own, so whatever structure appears in
the correlations must belong to the system. Cortex is not driven that way. Its input
arrives through a small set of ascending pathways, at particular places, with its own
timing and coordination. Once the input has structure, FC is no longer a property of the
cortex alone. It is what a particular input becomes after passing through a particular
medium on a particular folded sheet, and quite different combinations of the three produce
the same FC. Fitting FC therefore constrains the mechanism without identifying it. We
treat the fit as a constraint rather than a prize, and ask what the admissible mechanisms
do: how structured input, propagating as waves through a graded medium, converges, cancels
and steers to produce the patterns we measure. On this view the cortex behaves less like
an idling network than like a driven reservoir, and FC is the trace of what it does with
its input.
